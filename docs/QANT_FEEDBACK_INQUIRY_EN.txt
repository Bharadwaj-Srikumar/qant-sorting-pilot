Subject: Master's thesis - data paths, transfer characteristics, and possible access to the Q.ANT NPU

Dear Q.ANT team,

For my master's thesis in Computer Science at Bochum University of Applied
Sciences, supervised by Prof. Henrik Blunck, I am investigating mappings of
Bitonic and rank sorting onto hybrid photonic processors. The work is designed
to provide meaningful results through modelling and reproducible evaluation
even without hardware access.

Using your SDK 2.3.1 CPU backend, I have investigated linear difference
calculations and a comparator based on the periodic function. In the current
path, the host receives the difference d and computes u = u0 - alpha*d before
calling the periodic function. At that point, sign(d) already suffices for the
decision. I would therefore like to clarify whether a different execution path
could give the native nonlinearity a useful role in the complete computation.

An affine CPU control computes the phase within the linear call from
[a/2^w, b/2^w, 1] (w = key width), using weights [-pi/2, pi/2, pi/2]. At 8 bits,
this particular encoding produces 40 false ties among 65,536 ordered pairs
because the CPU backend rounds individual products to BF16. For example, 165
and 166 become indistinguishable. I treat this as a CPU finding and would
particularly like to clarify its relationship to actual device precision.

The following information would be especially helpful for a technical assessment:

1. Can MVM or an affine calculation and the periodic nonlinearity be connected
   within the NPU without returning the intermediate result to the host?
   Is a constant offset supported? Which internal electronic processing steps,
   buffers, and E/O and O/E conversions remain? Is there a documented interface
   for this execution path?

2. What are the actual transfer characteristics, input ranges, and resolutions
   of the periodic function? Are data available on channel differences, drift,
   and repeated outputs, especially near a decision boundary?

3. Are measurements or models of noise and quantization available, including
   the signal reference, measurement bandwidth, and operating conditions?
   How do the SDK's BF16 values map to physical signal ranges? Does rounding
   each MVM product in the CPU backend reflect the hardware, or is it only a
   software-modelling choice? What internal precision is used for products,
   accumulation, and the transition to the nonlinearity?

4. Which capacity, call, and transfer limits are documented for the linear
   and periodic operations? Can weights and intermediate results remain
   resident on the device?

5. Would limited research access be possible, or alternatively could suitable
   transfer-characteristic and noise data be provided?

A measurement plan and a transfer-characteristic acquisition script are ready.
I explicitly do not treat the existing CPU results as evidence of physical
transfer characteristics, optical cascading, or a runtime or energy advantage.

The public project repository is available at:
https://github.com/Bharadwaj-Srikumar/qant-sorting-pilot

Thank you for your support.

Kind regards,
Bharadwaj Srikumar
